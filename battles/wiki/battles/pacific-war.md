# The Pacific War

> The war in Asia and the Pacific began with Japan's invasion of China in July 1937 and widened on 7 December 1941, when Japan struck Pearl Harbor, Malaya and the Philippines. In five months Japan conquered Southeast Asia and the western Pacific. The Coral Sea, Midway, Guadalcanal and the Kokoda Track stopped it in 1942; from 1943 the Allies advanced across the Central Pacific and up New Guinea, retook Burma, destroyed the Japanese navy at the Philippine Sea and Leyte Gulf and burned Japan's cities from the air. After Iwo Jima and Okinawa, the atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the Soviet invasion of Manchuria brought Japan's surrender in August 1945. Most of the war's dead, well over 20 million people, were Chinese civilians.

This is the simulator's third war-scale scenario and the companion to [[world-war-ii]] (Europe). One map runs from the Bay of Bengal to Hawaii and from the Aleutians to northern Australia; units are armies, fleets, carrier groups and air forces, and fifteen phases run from the Marco Polo Bridge to the surrender.

## Background

Japan seized Manchuria in 1931 and set up the puppet state of Manchukuo. On 7 July 1937 a clash at the Marco Polo Bridge near Beijing became full-scale war with China. Japan joined the Axis in September 1940 and moved into French Indochina; the United States, Britain and the Netherlands answered with embargoes that cut off most of Japan's oil. Japan's leaders decided to seize the oil and rubber of Southeast Asia and to cripple the US Pacific Fleet first.

## Terrain

1 tile is about 250–300 km. Mainland Asia fills the north-west: the Tibetan plateau and the hills of Sichuan around Chongqing, the farmland of eastern China crossed by the Yangtze, the Gobi and the Manchurian plain, and the jungles of Burma, Indochina and Malaya. Japan, Taiwan, the Philippines and the islands of the East Indies line the western Pacific; New Guinea, with the Owen Stanley Range and the Kokoda Track across it, lies above Australia; the Solomons and Rabaul to its east. The Central Pacific atolls (the Gilberts, Marshalls, Marianas, Carolines, Midway, Wake, Iwo Jima and Okinawa) are single tiles in a vast ocean, with Pearl Harbor on the eastern edge. Ships move only on the sea; island assaults are separate units that come ashore on their island.

## Order of battle

**Allies.** China's National Revolutionary Army; the US Pacific Fleet's battle line and carrier task forces with their air groups; US and Philippine forces under MacArthur (1941–42); Malaya Command; Force Z; the ABDA fleet and the Netherlands East Indies Army; Burma Corps, then the Fourteenth Army; the Chinese Expeditionary Force in Burma; Australian and US forces in New Guinea and Kenney's air forces; the Marines and Army on Guadalcanal, Tarawa, the Marianas, Iwo Jima and Okinawa; the Sixth Army in the Philippines; the B-29s of XXI Bomber Command; and the Soviet Far East Command (August 1945).

**Japan.** The armies in China and the Kwantung Army in Manchuria; Nagumo's Kido Butai; the Combined Fleet's battleships and, from 1944, Ozawa's Mobile Fleet; the naval air arm and its kamikaze units; the 25th, 14th, 16th and 15th Armies of the southern conquests; the South Seas Detachment and 18th Army in New Guinea, the 17th Army on Guadalcanal and the 8th Area Army at Rabaul; the island garrisons of Tarawa, Saipan, Iwo Jima and Okinawa; and the home islands.

## Course of the battle

1. **The Second Sino-Japanese War, July 1937 – 1941.** The Marco Polo Bridge incident (7 July 1937); the battle of Shanghai from August; Nanjing taken in December 1937 and the Nanjing massacre (an estimated 200,000–300,000 victims); Wuhan falls in 1938 and the government moves to Chongqing.
2. **Pearl Harbor and the first strikes, 7–10 December 1941.** The attack on Pearl Harbor sinks four battleships, destroys 188 aircraft and kills 2,403 Americans for 29 Japanese aircraft; Japan lands in Malaya and attacks the Philippines; Prince of Wales and Repulse are sunk on 10 December; Hong Kong falls on 25 December.
3. **The southern conquests, December 1941 – May 1942.** Singapore surrenders on 15 February 1942 (about 80,000 prisoners); the Dutch East Indies on 8–9 March after the Java Sea battles; Bataan on 9 April and Corregidor on 6 May; Rangoon in March, and the British retreat to India. The Doolittle Raid strikes Tokyo on 18 April.
4. **Coral Sea and Midway, May – June 1942.** The Coral Sea (4–8 May) turns back the invasion of Port Moresby. At Midway (4–7 June) US dive-bombers sink Akagi, Kaga, Sōryū and Hiryū; Japan loses about 3,000 men and 248 aircraft, the US the carrier Yorktown and about 307 men. See [[carrier-warfare]].
5. **Guadalcanal, Kokoda and Milne Bay, July 1942 – February 1943.** The Marines land on Guadalcanal on 7 August 1942; after six months of fighting on land, at sea and in the air Japan evacuates 10,652 men by 9 February 1943. The South Seas Detachment comes within 32 km of Port Moresby on the Kokoda Track before it is turned back; Milne Bay is Japan's first defeat on land; Buna and Gona fall by 22 January 1943.
6. **Cartwheel, 1943.** The advance up the Solomons and New Guinea; Yamamoto is shot down over Bougainville on 18 April 1943; Rabaul is bypassed rather than assaulted ([[leapfrogging]]).
7. **Tarawa and the Central Pacific drive, November 1943 – February 1944.** Betio is taken on 20–23 November 1943: about 1,700 Americans and 4,690 Japanese and Korean defenders die, and 17 Japanese soldiers are captured. The Marshalls follow in February 1944.
8. **Imphal, Kohima and Ichi-Go, March – December 1944.** Japan's invasion of India fails at Imphal and Kohima (8 March – 3 July 1944), with about 55,000 Japanese casualties against about 12,600 Allied. In China Operation Ichi-Go (19 April – December 1944) takes the American air bases in the south.
9. **Saipan and the Philippine Sea, June – August 1944.** Saipan (15 June – 9 July 1944) and the Battle of the Philippine Sea (19–20 June), in which Japan loses three carriers and 550–645 aircraft. The fall of Saipan brings down Tojo's government and puts Tokyo in B-29 range.
10. **Leyte Gulf and the return to the Philippines, October 1944 – March 1945.** The Sixth Army lands on Leyte on 20 October; the Battle of Leyte Gulf (23–26 October) costs Japan 26 ships and sees the first organised [[kamikaze]] attacks. Luzon follows in January 1945 and Manila is destroyed in February–March.
11. **Burma reconquered, December 1944 – May 1945.** The Fourteenth Army crosses the Irrawaddy, takes Meiktila and Mandalay and reaches Rangoon on 3 May 1945, just ahead of the monsoon.
12. **Iwo Jima and the fire raids, February – May 1945.** Iwo Jima (19 February – 26 March) costs about 6,800 American dead and almost all of its 21,000 defenders. The B-29 incendiary raid on Tokyo on 9–10 March kills 90,000–100,000 people ([[strategic-bombing]]).
13. **Okinawa, 1 April – 22 June 1945.** The largest and bloodiest battle of the Pacific War: about 94,000–110,000 Japanese soldiers, about 12,500 Americans and 40,000–150,000 Okinawan civilians die; the battleship Yamato is sunk on 7 April.
14. **Atomic bombs and August Storm, 6–15 August 1945.** Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August) kill 150,000–246,000 people by the end of the year; the Soviet Union declares war on 8 August and invades Manchuria on 9 August; the Emperor announces the surrender on 15 August.
15. **Surrender, 15 August – 9 September 1945.** The instrument of surrender is signed on 2 September 1945 and the China Expeditionary Army surrenders at Nanjing on 9 September.

## Aftermath & significance

Japan was occupied by the Allies under MacArthur until 1952 and given a new constitution. Taiwan returned to China, Korea was divided at the 38th parallel, and the Soviet Union took southern Sakhalin and the Kurils. The Chinese Civil War resumed in 1946 and ended with the founding of the People's Republic in 1949. The European empires in Asia did not survive the war for long: the Philippines, India, Burma and Indonesia became independent within a few years. The war made the aircraft carrier the capital ship of every major navy.

## What-if levers

- **Pearl Harbor's third strike.** Nagumo withdrew without hitting the oil tanks, dry docks and repair shops; destroying them could have kept the Pacific Fleet out of action for far longer.
- **Midway's intelligence.** Without American codebreaking the Japanese carriers might have found the US carriers first.
- **Guadalcanal.** Committing reinforcements in strength in August 1942, instead of piecemeal, could have retaken Henderson Field.
- **Imphal.** Mutaguchi gambled on capturing British supplies; without that gamble the 15th Army would not have starved.
- **Leyte Gulf.** Had Kurita pressed on past Samar, he could have reached the landing fleet that Halsey had left exposed.
- **The end of the war.** An invasion of Japan (Operation Downfall) would have followed if Japan had not surrendered in August 1945.
