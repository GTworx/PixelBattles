# World War I

> The First World War (1914–1918) set the Entente of France, the British Empire, Russia, Italy, Serbia, Romania and, from 1917, the United States against the Central Powers of Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire and Bulgaria. Germany's attempt to knock out France in 1914 failed on the Marne, and the war in the west froze into trench lines from the Channel to Switzerland, while the fighting spread to Galicia, the Alps, the Dardanelles, Macedonia, the Caucasus, Sinai and Palestine, Mesopotamia and the Libyan desert. About 9–10 million soldiers died. It ended when the Central Powers collapsed one after another in the autumn of 1918, and with them the German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian and Ottoman empires.

This page is the simulator's one war-scale scenario: one map for every front, armies and army groups as units, and fifteen phases from the invasion of Belgium to the armistice of 11 November 1918.

## Background

The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 led Austria-Hungary to declare war on Serbia on 28 July. The alliance system turned the Balkan crisis into a general war within a week: Russia mobilised for Serbia, Germany declared war on Russia (1 August) and France (3 August), and its invasion of neutral Belgium brought Britain in on 4 August. The Ottoman Empire joined the Central Powers at the end of October 1914 and Bulgaria in October 1915; Italy joined the Entente in May 1915, Romania in August 1916, and the United States declared war on Germany in April 1917 after the resumption of unrestricted submarine warfare.

Germany's war plan (the Schlieffen Plan as modified by Moltke) put most of its army in the west to defeat France in about six weeks by a wheel through Belgium, holding East Prussia with one army until the French were beaten. France's Plan XVII sent its armies straight into Lorraine and the Ardennes. Neither side expected a long war.

## Terrain

The map runs from Ireland, Scotland and southern Scandinavia in the north to the Sahara, Sinai and the Persian Gulf in the south, and from Portugal across to Moscow, the Caucasus and Mesopotamia; one tile is about 100 km. It shows the North Sea, the Baltic, the Mediterranean and the Black Sea, the Alps, the Pyrenees, the Carpathians, the Balkan and Dinaric mountains, the Taurus, the Caucasus and the Zagros, the Pripet marshes and the Masurian lakes, the Marne, Rhine, Danube, Isonzo, Nile, Suez Canal, Jordan, Euphrates and Tigris, and the North African and Arabian deserts.

Every front fits on one grid, so the geography is compressed: the whole Western Front from the Channel to Switzerland is about seven tiles, and the Gallipoli peninsula is drawn as a two-tile spit cut off from Thrace by the Gulf of Saros and from Asia by the Dardanelles (the narrow neck at Bulair is left out so that the peninsula is a dead end, as it was for the landing force).

## Order of battle

Units are armies or army groups at theatre scale. Strengths are representative figures for the forces in each theatre, rounded, and change from phase to phase as armies are destroyed, rebuilt and reinforced.

**Entente.** In the west: the French 5th and 6th Armies (Lanrezac, Franchet d'Espèrey, Maunoury), the French centre armies (Joffre; Pétain at Verdun), the French 1st and 2nd Armies in Lorraine, the French artillery, the British Expeditionary Force (French, then Haig), British heavy artillery and the Tank Corps from 1916, and the American Expeditionary Forces (Pershing) from 1918. In the east: the Russian 1st Army (later the Northern and Western Fronts), the Russian 2nd Army (Samsonov) and the Southwestern Front (Ivanov, then Brusilov), the Russian Caucasus Army (Yudenich) and the Romanian Army. In the south: the Serbian Army (Putnik), the Italian Army (Cadorna, then Diaz), the Allied Army of the Orient at Salonika, the Mediterranean Expeditionary Force and the Anglo-French fleet at Gallipoli, the British forces in Egypt and the Egyptian Expeditionary Force (Murray, then Allenby), the Desert Mounted Corps (Chauvel), the Western Frontier Force, the Arab Northern Army (Faisal, with T. E. Lawrence), Indian Expeditionary Force D (Townshend) and the Tigris Corps (later Maude's army). At sea: the Grand Fleet (Jellicoe, Beatty).

**Central Powers.** In the west: the German 1st and 2nd Armies (von Kluck, von Bülow), the 3rd–5th Armies (Crown Prince Wilhelm), the 6th and 7th Armies (Rupprecht of Bavaria), the heavy siege artillery, and in 1918 the storm armies of the Spring Offensive. In the east: the German 8th Army and Ober Ost (Hindenburg and Ludendorff), Army Group Mackensen, the 9th Army (Falkenhayn) and the Austro-Hungarian armies in Galicia (Conrad). In the south: the Austro-Hungarian Balkan armies, the 5th (Isonzo) Army (Boroević), the Austro-German 14th Army at Caporetto (von Below), the Bulgarian Army, the Ottoman 5th Army at Gallipoli (Liman von Sanders, with Mustafa Kemal), the Dardanelles forts, the Ottoman 3rd Army in the Caucasus (Enver Pasha), the 4th Army in Sinai and Palestine (Djemal, Kress von Kressenstein, later the Yıldırım Army Group), the forces in Mesopotamia (later the 6th Army) and the Senussi of Cyrenaica. At sea: the High Seas Fleet (Scheer, Hipper).

## Course of the battle

1. **Invasion of Belgium and the Battle of the Frontiers, 4–24 August 1914.** German heavy siege guns reduce the Liège forts and the right wing wheels through Belgium; the French offensives in Lorraine and the Ardennes are repulsed with enormous losses (27,000 French killed on 22 August); the BEF fights at Mons. Russia invades East Prussia; Serbia beats the first Austro-Hungarian invasion at Cer; the armies clash in Galicia.
2. **The Great Retreat and Tannenberg, 24 August – 5 September 1914.** The French and British fall back toward Paris. Hindenburg and Ludendorff encircle the Russian 2nd Army at Tannenberg (about 92,000 prisoners). The Russians beat the Austro-Hungarians in Galicia and take Lemberg.
3. **The Marne, 5–12 September 1914.** Joffre's counter-offensive and the gap between the German 1st and 2nd Armies force the Germans back to the Aisne. Moltke is replaced by Falkenhayn; the Russian 1st Army is driven out of East Prussia at the Masurian Lakes.
4. **The race to the sea and First Ypres, September – December 1914.** The lines extend to the Channel; the BEF holds Ypres. The Ottoman Empire enters the war; British and Indian troops land at Basra; Serbia expels the second Austro-Hungarian invasion at the Kolubara.
5. **Sarikamish, the Suez raid and the Carpathians, December 1914 – February 1915.** Enver's winter offensive at Sarikamish destroys his own 3rd Army; the Ottoman attack on the Suez Canal fails; Austro-Hungarian winter offensives in the Carpathians fail and Przemyśl falls in March.
6. **Gallipoli, Gorlice–Tarnów and the Isonzo, March – September 1915.** The naval attack on the Dardanelles fails on 18 March; the landings of 25 April are held on the beaches. Mackensen's breakthrough at Gorlice–Tarnów starts the Russian Great Retreat (Warsaw falls on 5 August). Italy enters the war and attacks on the Isonzo. Gas is used at Second Ypres.
7. **Serbia overrun, Salonika, Ctesiphon and the Senussi, October 1915 – January 1916.** Bulgaria joins the Central Powers; Serbia is overrun and its army retreats through Albania. An Allied force lands at Salonika. Townshend is stopped at Ctesiphon; the Senussi rise on the Egyptian frontier. Gallipoli is evacuated.
8. **Verdun, Erzurum and Kut, February – May 1916.** The German offensive at Verdun begins on 21 February; Pétain holds the city. Yudenich takes Erzurum. The Senussi are beaten at Agagia. Kut surrenders on 29 April after a 147-day siege.
9. **Jutland, the Brusilov offensive and the Somme, May – August 1916.** Jutland (31 May – 1 June) leaves the British in command of the North Sea. The Brusilov offensive shatters the Austro-Hungarian front. The Somme opens on 1 July with 57,470 British casualties on the first day. Romania enters the war; the Ottoman attack at Romani fails.
10. **Romania, Verdun regained, Baghdad, September 1916 – March 1917.** Falkenhayn and Mackensen overrun Romania (Bucharest falls 6 December). Tanks go into action at Flers. The French retake Douaumont. Maude takes Baghdad on 11 March 1917.
11. **Revolution, America and Gaza, March – July 1917.** The Tsar abdicates; the United States declares war. Arras and Vimy Ridge; the Nivelle offensive fails and the French army mutinies. Two British attacks on Gaza fail. The Kerensky offensive collapses; the Arab army takes Aqaba.
12. **Passchendaele, Caporetto, Beersheba and Jerusalem, July – December 1917.** Third Ypres ends in the mud of Passchendaele; massed tanks break in at Cambrai. Caporetto drives the Italians back to the Piave. The Light Horse charge at Beersheba; Allenby takes Jerusalem. Russia signs an armistice.
13. **Brest-Litovsk and the Kaiserschlacht, March – July 1918.** Russia and Romania make peace. The German Spring Offensive breaks the British line on the Somme and reaches the Marne; Foch becomes supreme Allied commander; the Americans fight at Cantigny and Belleau Wood; the last Austro-Hungarian offensive fails on the Piave.
14. **The Hundred Days, Megiddo and Dobro Pole, July – September 1918.** The Second Marne and Amiens (8 August) begin the Allied advance; the Americans clear St Mihiel and attack in the Meuse–Argonne. Dobro Pole knocks Bulgaria out (armistice 29 September). Megiddo destroys the Ottoman armies in Palestine.
15. **The armistices, October – 11 November 1918.** Damascus and Aleppo fall; the Ottoman army in Mesopotamia surrenders at Sharqat and the Ottoman Empire signs the Armistice of Mudros (30 October). Vittorio Veneto ends Austria-Hungary's war (Villa Giusti, 3 November). The German navy mutinies at Kiel, the Kaiser abdicates, and the Armistice of Compiègne takes effect at 11 a.m. on 11 November.

Entente victory. Civilian deaths from fighting, famine, massacre (including the Armenian genocide) and disease added several million more; the influenza pandemic of 1918–1920 killed far more again.

## Aftermath & significance

The Paris Peace Conference produced the treaties of Versailles (Germany), Saint-Germain (Austria), Trianon (Hungary), Neuilly (Bulgaria) and Sèvres (the Ottoman Empire, later replaced by Lausanne after the Turkish War of Independence led by Mustafa Kemal). Four empires fell; Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia and the Baltic states appeared; the Ottoman Arab provinces became British and French mandates. The war introduced tanks, aircraft, poison gas and submarine warfare on a large scale, and its settlement shaped the causes of the Second World War (see [[stalingrad]]).

## What-if levers

- **The Marne.** Had von Kluck gone west of Paris as planned, or kept contact with von Bülow, the gap the BEF and the French 5th Army exploited might never have opened.
- **Gallipoli.** A combined naval and land attack in February–March 1915, before the Ottoman 5th Army was in place, might have opened the Dardanelles and the sea route to Russia.
- **Verdun.** Falkenhayn's offensive bled the German army almost as much as the French; holding back would have left him a reserve for the Somme.
- **Brusilov's follow-up.** If the other Russian fronts had attacked with Brusilov in June 1916, Austria-Hungary might have collapsed two years early.
- **The Spring Offensive.** Ludendorff spread his 1918 offensives across several sectors instead of driving for Amiens and the British–French junction.
- **America.** Without unrestricted U-boat warfare the United States might have stayed out, and the German 1918 offensives would have met no fresh army.
