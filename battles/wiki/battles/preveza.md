# Battle of Preveza

> On 28 September 1538 the Ottoman fleet of [[hayreddin-barbarossa]], which had sheltered inside the Gulf of Arta, came out against the much larger armada of the Holy League (Venice, the Papacy, Habsburg Spain, Genoa and the Knights of Malta) under [[andrea-doria]]. After three days of stand-off at the mouth of the gulf and a day of manoeuvre in which the becalmed Christian sailing ships took most of the fighting, the divided League fleet withdrew to Corfu in the night. The Ottomans lost no ships. The victory, followed by Djerba in 1560, gave them command of the Mediterranean until Lepanto in 1571.

## Background

In 1537 Barbarossa, Kapudan Pasha (grand admiral) of Suleiman the Magnificent since 1533, took a string of Venetian islands (Syros, Aegina, Ios, Paros, Tinos, Karpathos, Kasos and Naxos), besieged Venetian Corfu without success and raided the Spanish coast of Calabria. In February 1538 Pope Paul III assembled a Holy League of the Papal States, Venice, Habsburg Spain, Genoa and the Knights of Malta. Charles V's admiral, the Genoese Andrea Doria, was given command; he had helped drive Barbarossa from Tunis in 1535.

The Papal fleet under Marco Grimani and the Venetian fleet under Vincenzo Cappello arrived first. Grimani tried to land troops near the fortress of Preveza and was driven off by Ottoman forces under Murat Reis, falling back on Corfu. Barbarossa, then at Kos, took Kefalonia and brought his fleet to Preveza. Doria joined the Venetians and the Papal fleet on 22 September; the combined fleet gathered at Corfu on 23 September. Secret contacts between Doria and Barbarossa about Barbarossa entering the Emperor's service came to nothing.

## Terrain

Preveza stands at the tip of a peninsula on the north side of the strait that joins the Ionian Sea to the Gulf of Arta; Actium, where Octavian beat Antony and Cleopatra in 31 BC, faces it on the south side. The mouth is a narrow bottleneck under the fortress guns, and the whole shore of the gulf was Ottoman, so a fleet inside it could wait in safety. Outside lies the open Ionian Sea, where an autumn wind could drive sailing ships onto a hostile coast. About 30 miles south lie Lefkada and the Sessola islets, where the fighting of 28 September took place.

## Order of battle

**Ottoman.** On 28 September Barbarossa came out in a Y-shaped line: himself in the centre with Sinan Reis, Cafer Reis, Şaban Reis and his son Hasan Reis; Seydi Ali Reis on the left; Salih Reis on the right; and Turgut Reis with Murat Reis, Güzelce Mehmet Reis and Sadık Reis commanding a rear wing of six large galliots. (The Hospitaller account puts Dragut in front.)

**Holy League.** Doria's Spanish and Genoese galleys, Cappello's Venetian galleys, Grimani's Papal galleys and Simeone's few Hospitaller galleys, with a separate body of sailing ships (carracks and galleons) under Francesco Doria, including five Spanish and Genoese ships under Francisco Sarmiento, galleons under Juan Villegas de Figueroa and Machín de Munguía, and the great Venetian galleon (the "Galeone di Venezia") under Alessandro Condalmiero. The three accounts give three different orders for these squadrons.

## Course of the battle

The accounts are, in Roger Merriman's words, almost impossible to reconcile: Venetian and Papal writers blame Doria, Spanish and Imperial writers blame the Venetians and the Pope's men, and the Hospitaller account blames the lack of a unified command. The phases below follow what they share.

1. **Grimani's landing, early September.** The Papal fleet's attempt to land near the Preveza fortress is caught by Murat Reis and repulsed; Grimani falls back on Corfu.
2. **Barbarossa reaches Preveza, mid-September.** Coming from Kos by way of Kefalonia, Barbarossa takes the main fleet into the Gulf of Arta.
3. **The League arrives, 22–25 September.** Doria joins Cappello and Grimani on 22 September; on 25 September the armada takes station in front of the mouth of the gulf.
4. **Stand-off, 25–27 September.** Barbarossa cannot come out without being overwhelmed; Doria cannot force the bottleneck and fears for his sailing ships on a lee shore. A Spanish marine landing to silence the Ottoman guns is rejected, partly because of Grimani's failure and partly because no safe beach is found. On Sinan Reis's advice, which Barbarossa at first resisted, Ottoman troops land at Actium; artillery fire stops their attempt to entrench, the only exchange of those days.
5. **Doria turns south, night of 27 September.** To lure Barbarossa out, Doria sails about 30 miles south toward Sessola, near Lefkada, threatening Lepanto. The galleys pull ahead of the sailing ships.
6. **Barbarossa sorties, 28 September.** The Ottoman fleet comes out in its Y formation, keeping close to its own shore. Doria turns back toward it.
7. **The becalmed sailing ships.** The wind fails and the Christian sailing ships lie immobile, separated from the galleys. Ottoman galleys attack them. Condalmiero's Venetian galleon, stranded about four miles offshore, fights off repeated attacks with its heavy guns, even after Barbarossa's flagship joins in, and is damaged but not taken. Villegas de Figueroa's galleon is overwhelmed and sinks after a last stand by its soldiers; Munguía's ship is dismasted but escapes; Sarmiento's five ships hold their own.
8. **Doria's feints.** Doria twice makes as if to attack, hoping to draw Barbarossa away from the coast; Barbarossa does not commit. In the Venetian account Cappello and Grimani board Doria's flagship to demand battle; in the Spanish account Doria orders three attacks and the Venetian and Papal galleys do not move.
9. **The night withdrawal.** Before nightfall Doria orders the fleet away. With rain falling and lanterns extinguished, the League withdraws toward Corfu.

## Aftermath & significance

Suleiman celebrated the victory and rewarded Barbarossa's officers. The League's members accused each other: Cappello charged Doria with deliberately sparing the Venetians' enemy, and Virginio Orsini accused him of collusion with Barbarossa. The League took Castelnuovo (Herceg Novi), but quarrelled over it, and Barbarossa retook it in 1539. In October 1540 Venice made a separate peace, giving up its remaining possessions in the Peloponnese, Dalmatia and the islands and paying 300,000 ducats. With Preveza and Djerba (1560), the Ottomans were the leading naval power in the Mediterranean until Lepanto in 1571. The galleons' firepower against galleys at Preveza also pushed western shipbuilding toward heavily gunned ships such as the galleass ([[galley-warfare]]).

## What-if levers

- **A unified command.** With one admiral obeyed by all the contingents, the League's numbers could have been brought to bear at once.
- **Forcing the mouth or landing.** The rejected Spanish marine landing might have silenced the Preveza batteries and trapped Barbarossa in the gulf.
- **The wind.** Had the wind held on 28 September, the League's sailing ships, with their heavy broadside guns, could have fought the galleys on their own terms.
- **Relieving the sailing ships.** If Doria's galleys had rowed to the becalmed ships' support, the Ottoman galleys attacking them would have been caught between two fires.
- **Barbarossa leaving the shore.** Had Barbarossa taken Doria's bait and come far out to sea, the battle would have been fought where the League's numbers counted most.
