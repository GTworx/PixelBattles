# World War II in Europe

> The Second World War in Europe (1939–1945) set Nazi Germany and its allies (Fascist Italy until 1943, Romania, Hungary, Finland and others) against Poland, France and the British Empire from 1939, the Soviet Union from June 1941 and the United States from December 1941. German armoured campaigns overran Poland, France and the Balkans in under two years and carried the Wehrmacht to the gates of Moscow and the Volga. The Battle of Britain, Stalingrad, El Alamein, the Atlantic and Kursk turned the war; the Allies then invaded Italy and Normandy and bombed German industry, while the Red Army destroyed the German armies in the east and took Berlin. Germany surrendered on 7–8 May 1945. It was the deadliest conflict in history, and most of its dead were civilians, among them about six million Jews murdered in the Holocaust.

This page is the simulator's second war-scale scenario, on the same Europe and North Africa map as [[world-war-i]]: armies, army groups, fleets and air forces as units, and fifteen phases from the invasion of Poland to the surrender. The war against Japan in Asia and the Pacific is outside the map and appears only in the narration.

## Background

The peace of 1919 left Germany resentful and the European order fragile. Hitler came to power in 1933, rearmed Germany, remilitarised the Rhineland (1936), annexed Austria (March 1938) and the Sudetenland (Munich, September 1938) and occupied the rest of Czechoslovakia in March 1939. Britain and France then guaranteed Poland. On 23 August 1939 the Nazi–Soviet pact divided eastern Europe between Germany and the Soviet Union, and on 1 September Germany invaded Poland; Britain and France declared war on 3 September.

## Terrain

The map is the [[world-war-i]] map (1 tile ≈ 100 km), from Ireland and Scandinavia to the Sahara and from the Atlantic to the Volga, with Stalingrad and the lower Volga added on the eastern edge. It shows the North European Plain, which gave the panzers open ground from the Low Countries to Moscow; the Ardennes forest north of the Maginot Line, where Army Group A broke through in 1940; the Channel and the North Sea; the Pripet marshes; the Alps and the long Italian peninsula with its defensive lines; and the narrow coastal strip of the Libyan and Egyptian desert, where the armies moved along a single road between the sea and the sand. Geography is compressed: the whole Normandy campaign spans a few tiles, and Stalingrad is one.

## Order of battle

**Allies.** Polish Army (1939); French Army with the 1st Army Group, and the British Expeditionary Force (1940); RAF Fighter Command; the Royal Navy's Home Fleet and Western Approaches escorts; the Western Desert Force, then Eighth Army; the Soviet fronts (Northwestern/Leningrad, Western/1st Belorussian, Southwestern and Southern, Stalingrad, Southwestern and Don for Uranus, Central and Voronezh/1st Ukrainian); the Torch armies and British First Army in Tunisia; the 15th Army Group (US Seventh/Fifth and British Eighth Armies) in Sicily and Italy; RAF Bomber Command and the US Eighth Air Force; the Neptune invasion fleet; the 21st and US 12th Army Groups; and Patton's US Third Army.

**Axis.** The three German army groups (in 1939–41 Bock's North/B/Centre, Rundstedt's South/A/South and Leeb's C/North); the Luftwaffe; the Kriegsmarine and U-boat arm; the Italian 10th Army in Libya; Rommel's Afrika Korps and Panzer Army Africa; OB West and Army Group B in France; Paulus's 6th Army with Hoth's 4th Panzer Army; the Citadel armies at Kursk; Kesselring's Army Group C in Italy; and the Reich's air defences and Ruhr industry.

## Course of the battle

1. **The invasion of Poland, 1 September – 6 October 1939.** Germany attacks from three sides; the Soviet Union invades from the east on 17 September. Warsaw surrenders on 27 September.
2. **Phoney War, Winter War and Norway, October 1939 – April 1940.** Little happens in the west. The Soviet Union attacks Finland (30 November 1939 – 12 March 1940). On 9 April 1940 Germany occupies Denmark and invades Norway.
3. **The fall of France, 10 May – 25 June 1940.** The [[blitzkrieg]] through the Ardennes crosses the Meuse at Sedan on 13 May and reaches the Channel. About 338,000 British and French troops are evacuated from Dunkirk by 4 June. Italy declares war on 10 June; Paris falls on 14 June; the armistice is signed on 22 June.
4. **The Battle of Britain, 10 July – 31 October 1940.** [[hugh-dowding]]'s Fighter Command defeats the Luftwaffe's bid for air superiority; the night Blitz begins on 7 September and the invasion is postponed.
5. **The desert war and the Balkans, September 1940 – June 1941.** Operation Compass destroys the Italian 10th Army (about 133,000 prisoners). [[erwin-rommel]] arrives in February 1941 and besieges Tobruk. Germany conquers Yugoslavia, Greece and Crete (April–May 1941).
6. **Operation Barbarossa, 22 June – December 1941.** About 3.8 million Axis troops invade the Soviet Union; Leningrad is besieged from 8 September; the Kiev pocket takes over half a million prisoners. The Germans are stopped before Moscow and [[georgy-zhukov]] counterattacks on 5 December. After Pearl Harbor Germany declares war on the United States (11 December).
7. **Case Blue, Gazala and the Atlantic, January – July 1942.** Germany drives toward the Caucasus and Stalingrad. Rommel takes Tobruk on 21 June and is stopped at the first battle of El Alamein in July. The U-boats reach the height of their success.
8. **Stalingrad and El Alamein, August – November 1942.** The 6th Army fights its way into Stalingrad from 23 August against [[vasily-chuikov]]'s 62nd Army. [[bernard-montgomery]] wins the Second Battle of El Alamein (23 October – 4 November), and Operation Torch lands in North Africa on 8 November.
9. **Operation Uranus, Tunisia and Black May, November 1942 – May 1943.** The 6th Army is encircled ([[operation-uranus]]) and surrenders on 31 January – 2 February 1943 with about 91,000 prisoners. Axis forces in Tunisia surrender on 13 May with over 250,000 men. In May 1943 the U-boats are defeated in the Atlantic.
10. **Kursk, Sicily and the bomber offensive, July – August 1943.** Operation Citadel (5 July) fails against the Soviet defence in depth; the Allies land on Sicily on 9–10 July and Mussolini falls on 25 July. [[strategic-bombing]] of German cities and industry intensifies.
11. **Italy, the Dnieper and Leningrad relieved, September 1943 – May 1944.** Italy's armistice (8 September) and the Salerno landing; the Gustav Line and Monte Cassino (January–May 1944). Kiev is freed on 6 November 1943; the siege of Leningrad is lifted in January 1944.
12. **D-Day, Bagration and the liberation of Paris, June – August 1944.** Rome falls on 4 June. On 6 June the Allies land in Normandy under [[dwight-d-eisenhower]]; Operation Cobra and the Falaise pocket follow, and Paris is liberated on 25 August. Operation Bagration (from 22–23 June) destroys most of Army Group Centre.
13. **Market Garden, the Bulge and the Vistula–Oder, September 1944 – January 1945.** The Arnhem airborne operation fails (17–25 September); the German Ardennes offensive (16 December 1944 – January 1945) is beaten back; the Vistula–Oder offensive (from 12 January 1945) carries the Red Army to the Oder.
14. **Across the Rhine and the Battle of Berlin, March – 2 May 1945.** Remagen (7 March), the Rhine crossings and the Ruhr pocket (about 300,000 prisoners). The Soviet assault on Berlin from 16 April; American and Soviet troops meet at Torgau on 25 April; Hitler kills himself on 30 April; Berlin surrenders on 2 May, as do the German armies in Italy.
15. **Surrender, 2 – 8 May 1945.** Jodl signs the unconditional surrender at Reims on 7 May and Keitel in Berlin on 8 May (VE Day). Japan surrenders on 2 September 1945.

## Aftermath & significance

Germany was occupied and divided into four zones, its leaders tried at Nuremberg. The Soviet Union kept eastern Europe, and the line where the Allied armies met became the boundary of the Cold War. The war ended the European empires' dominance and left the United States and the Soviet Union as superpowers; the United Nations was founded in 1945.

## What-if levers

- **The halt before Dunkirk.** Had the panzers pushed on in late May 1940, the BEF might not have escaped.
- **The Battle of Britain.** A Luftwaffe that kept hammering Fighter Command's airfields instead of turning on London in September might have won air superiority over the Channel.
- **Barbarossa's priorities.** Turning Guderian south to Kiev in August 1941 won a vast encirclement but delayed the drive on Moscow until the autumn mud and winter.
- **Stalingrad.** Letting the 6th Army break out before the ring closed in November 1942 could have saved it.
- **Kursk.** Attacking in May, before the Soviet defences were complete, rather than waiting for new tanks until July.
- **D-Day weather.** Eisenhower's decision to go on 6 June in a gap in the storm; the next suitable tides were two weeks later.
