# Imperial Guard

## Definition

Napoleon's elite household troops, organized into the Old, Middle and Young Guard, with their own infantry, cavalry and artillery; the army's strategic reserve.

## How it worked

The Guard was held back and committed only to decide a battle, so it carried huge moral weight: the French believed it unbeatable, and its defeat could unnerve the whole army. At Waterloo part of it (the Young Guard and some Old Guard battalions) was drawn off to Plancenoit against the Prussians, so fewer Guard battalions were left to exploit the centre. At about 19:00-19:30 the Guard was sent against Wellington's right-centre and was repulsed by Maitland's Guards, the 52nd Foot and Chassé's Dutch division.

## Where it appears

- [[waterloo]]: the repulse of the Guard triggered the French rout; Old Guard squares covered the retreat near La Belle Alliance. See [[napoleon-bonaparte]].
