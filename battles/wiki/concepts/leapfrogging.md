# Leapfrogging (island hopping)

## Definition

The Allied amphibious strategy in the Pacific of bypassing heavily held islands and leaving their garrisons cut off from supply, instead of capturing every island in turn.

## How it worked

Air and sea power isolated a strongpoint so that it no longer mattered; the next landing went to a weakly held island farther on, which then became a base for the following jump. It saved time and lives, but bypassed garrisons did not always wither: some grew their own food, counterattacked, and tied down Allied divisions to watch them. MacArthur's Hollandia and Aitape landings in 1944 are the classic examples; in the Central Pacific, Nimitz still had to assault Tarawa, Saipan, Iwo Jima and Okinawa directly, at heavy cost.

## Where it appears

- [[pacific-war]]: Rabaul and its roughly 90,000 men were bypassed in 1943 rather than assaulted; the drives up New Guinea and across the Central Pacific islands.
