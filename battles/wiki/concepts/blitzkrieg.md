# Blitzkrieg

## Definition

"Lightning war": the popular name for the German armoured campaigns of 1939–41, in which concentrated tanks, motorised infantry and aircraft broke through at a chosen point and drove deep before the enemy could react.

## How it worked

Panzer divisions with radios in every tank were massed at the Schwerpunkt, the point of main effort, supported by dive-bombers in place of slow artillery; commanders were given objectives rather than detailed orders, and the breakthrough was exploited into encirclement battles (Kesselschlacht). Historians debate whether it was a doctrine at all: the term came from Western journalism, Hitler disowned it, and in 1939–40 only about a tenth of the German army was motorised.

## Where it appears

- [[world-war-ii]]: Poland in 1939, the Ardennes and Sedan in 1940 (see [[heinz-guderian]]), the Kiev and Vyazma encirclements of 1941; it failed against the prepared defences at Kursk in 1943 and was turned against the Germans in Operation Bagration in 1944. See also [[combined-arms]] and [[encirclement]].
