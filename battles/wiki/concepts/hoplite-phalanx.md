# Hoplite phalanx

## Definition

The close-order formation of Greek [[hoplite]] spearmen, shield to shield in ranks eight deep or so, that dominated Greek warfare from roughly the 7th century BCE.

## How it worked

Each man's round shield (hoplon) covered his left side and his neighbour's right, so the formation advanced as a wall of bronze and spear points. Success depended on cohesion and the push (othismos) of the rear ranks. It was powerful on flat or narrow ground and weak on flanks and rear, and against missile troops if it could not close.

## Where it appears

- [[marathon]]: the Athenians and Plataeans, with a thin centre and deep wings, broke the lighter Persian infantry and then enveloped the Persian centre ([[double-envelopment]]).
- [[thermopylae]]: in the narrow pass the phalanx faced Persian infantry with shorter spears and wicker shields, whose numbers could not be brought to bear ([[defile-defence]]).

- [[gaugamela]]: about 2,000 Greek mercenary hoplites stood in the Persian centre by [[darius-iii]], and were driven back by the [[macedonian-phalanx]].
