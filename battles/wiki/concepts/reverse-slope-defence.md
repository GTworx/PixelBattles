# Reverse slope defence

## Definition

Deploying a defending force behind the crest of a ridge, on the side facing away from the enemy, so that it is hidden from direct observation and fire until the attackers climb to the crest.

## How it worked

Artillery that cannot see its target cannot easily hit it, and an attacking column that reaches the crest meets fresh, intact infantry at close range. At Waterloo Wellington placed most of his army behind the Mont-Saint-Jean ridge, "out of sight of the enemy" (as the National Army Museum puts it, the term itself is modern), with strongpoints at Hougoumont, La Haye Sainte and Papelotte forward of it. Skirmishers held the crest.

## Where it appears

- [[waterloo]]: the half-hour bombardment by 80 French guns was less effective because Wellington's troops sat behind the slopes, and d'Erlon's columns were met by British volleys when they reached the ridge. See also [[defensive-terrain]].
