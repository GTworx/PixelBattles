# Strategic bombing

## Definition

Bombing an enemy's cities, industry and transport to destroy its ability and will to fight, rather than its forces in the field.

## How it worked

In the Second World War the Luftwaffe's Blitz struck British cities in 1940–41. RAF Bomber Command turned to night area bombing after the 1941 Butt Report showed how few bombs hit their targets; the US Eighth Air Force bombed by day against precise targets, suffering heavy losses (Schweinfurt, 1943) until long-range escort fighters such as the P-51 arrived. Accuracy was rarely better than a few miles. Attacks on oil and transport in the last year of the war were the most effective; German air-raid deaths are estimated at about 353,000–434,000.

## Where it appears

- [[world-war-ii]]: the Blitz, the bombing of the Ruhr and Hamburg (1943) and Dresden (February 1945); the bomber offensive also drew the Luftwaffe's fighters and much of Germany's artillery into home air defence.
