# Infantry square

## Definition

A hollow rectangular formation in which infantry face outward on all four sides, with bayonets fixed, to defeat cavalry attacking from any direction.

## How it worked

Horses will not charge a solid hedge of bayonets, and a steady square has no flank or rear to hit. The price is that a square is a dense, static target for artillery and skirmishers, so it works only when cavalry cannot be supported by guns and foot. At Waterloo the Allied infantry formed squares (often battalion-sized, in chequerboard formation) between their batteries; gunners fired on the cavalry as it approached and took shelter inside the squares as it came on.

## Where it appears

- [[waterloo]]: Ney's massed cavalry attacks at about 16:00 failed to break a square, though French guns and skirmishers pouring fire into the squares after La Haye Sainte fell nearly did what the horse could not. See also [[heavy-cavalry]].
