# Scythed chariots

## Definition

Light horse-drawn chariots fitted with blades projecting from the wheel hubs, the yoke or the body, intended to cut through close-packed infantry. Used by the Achaemenid Persians.

## How it worked

They needed flat, cleared ground and an enemy that did not open its ranks. Alexander's men drilled countermeasures: javelins against horses, pulling drivers down, beating shields to spook teams, and opening gaps in the phalanx (Warfare History Network; Arrian). At Gaugamela [[darius-iii]] had the plain levelled specifically for them.

## Where it appears

- [[gaugamela]]: about 200 chariots were sent against the Macedonian line; javelin-men and archers killed many of the teams, the ranks opened for the rest, and grooms and hypaspists finished them. The attack failed.
