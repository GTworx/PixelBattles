# Macedonian phalanx

## Definition

The infantry formation of Philip II and [[alexander-the-great]]: pezhetairoi ("foot companions") carrying the sarissa, a pike of some four to six metres, with a small shield slung from the neck. Organised in brigades, with the hypaspists as an elite corps on its right. (Sarissa length and brigade size here are general knowledge, not from the consulted sources.)

## How it worked

Warfare History Network notes that Persian infantry could not break the pikes at Gaugamela. It was less rigid than the older [[hoplite-phalanx]]. It was the anvil: it pinned the enemy centre while the cavalry, led by Alexander, struck from the flank. Its weakness was the gaps that opened when the line pivoted or stretched.

## Where it appears

- [[gaugamela]]: advanced obliquely behind Alexander's right, held against Persian horse, was stretched by Simmias's section lagging behind the wedge, and a reserve line behind it turned about to ride down raiders who reached the camp.
