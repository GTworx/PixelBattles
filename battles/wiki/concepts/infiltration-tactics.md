# Infiltration tactics (storm troops)

## Definition

Attacking a trench system with small, heavily armed assault groups that bypass strongpoints and push deep into the defences, leaving the strongpoints to follow-on troops, instead of advancing in waves on a broad front.

## How it worked

The German army developed the method from 1915 (Captain Willy Rohr's assault detachment) and spread it to whole "attack divisions" by 1918. A short, intense bombardment planned by Georg Bruchmüller, with gas and smoke shell, replaced the week-long barrage that warned the enemy. Storm troops with light machine guns, grenades, flamethrowers and light mortars then moved through gaps under cover of a creeping barrage, while the infantry behind mopped up. It broke the Russian line at Riga (September 1917), the Italian line at Caporetto (October 1917) and the British Fifth Army on the Somme on 21 March 1918, but the attackers outran their artillery and supplies and could not turn the break-ins into a decisive victory.

## Where it appears

- [[world-war-i]]: Caporetto (the Austro-German 14th Army) and the 1918 Spring Offensive planned by [[erich-ludendorff]].
