# Carrier warfare

## Definition

Naval warfare in which aircraft flown from carriers, rather than the guns of battleships, decide the battle, often between fleets that never see each other.

## How it worked

Carrier strike groups searched for the enemy with scout aircraft and attacked with dive-bombers and torpedo planes at ranges of hundreds of kilometres; finding the enemy first was decisive. Japan concentrated six carriers in one striking force for Pearl Harbor. The Coral Sea in May 1942 was the first battle fought entirely by carrier aircraft. Since the Second World War the carrier has replaced the battleship as the fleet's capital ship.

## Where it appears

- [[pacific-war]]: Pearl Harbor (December 1941); the Coral Sea and Midway (May–June 1942), where four Japanese fleet carriers were sunk in one day; the Philippine Sea (June 1944), the largest carrier battle, which destroyed Japan's carrier air power; and Leyte Gulf, where Japan's last carriers served as a decoy.
