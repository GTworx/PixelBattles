# Encirclement (Kessel)

## Definition

Cutting an army off from its supply and escape routes by closing a ring around it (German: _Kessel_, "cauldron"), then reducing the pocket.

## How it worked

A ring is closed by two pincers meeting behind the enemy; the trapped army is then starved, bombarded and squeezed while an outer front keeps relief out. At Stalingrad the ring closed at Kalach on 23 November 1942, trapping about 250,000 Axis men by Paulus's estimate. Hitler forbade a breakout and Göring promised 300 tons a day by air; about 90 arrived; Operation Winter Storm failed about 48 km short; Operation Ring (10 January) squeezed the pocket until the 6th Army surrendered on 31 January and 2 February 1943.
