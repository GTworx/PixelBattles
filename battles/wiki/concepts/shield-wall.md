# Shield wall

## Definition

A close formation of infantry whose shields overlap to form a continuous barrier, used across early medieval Europe.

## How it worked

At Hastings the English stood several ranks deep on the crest of a steep slope with woods and marsh covering the flanks. Shields and weapons stopped arrows and infantry assaults; the formation was strong only while it stayed closed, which is why the Norman feigned flights, which tempted men out of the line, worked.

## Where it appears

- [[hastings]]: Harold held the Senlac ridge in a dense line for most of the day; the line contracted as the housecarls were thinned out and was broken only at dusk.
