# Hugh Dowding

## Summary

British air chief marshal who led RAF Fighter Command from 1936 to November 1940 and won the Battle of Britain.

## Career

Born in Moffat, Scotland. A pilot and squadron commander in the First World War, he was the Air Ministry's member for supply and research in 1930–35, backing radar and the new monoplane fighters, and took over Fighter Command in July 1936. He built the "Dowding system" of radar, the Observer Corps, operations rooms and radio-directed fighters, resisted sending more fighter squadrons to France in 1940, and directed the defence in the Battle of Britain. He was replaced in November 1940.

## Command style

Prudent and economical: he conserved his fighters, rotated tired squadrons and fought a battle of attrition the Luftwaffe could not win, against critics who favoured massed "Big Wing" formations.
