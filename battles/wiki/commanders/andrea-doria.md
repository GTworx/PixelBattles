# Andrea Doria

## Summary

Genoese condottiero and admiral, the most famous Christian sea commander of his time, who led the Holy League fleet to its defeat at Preveza in 1538.

## Career

Orphaned young, he served as a soldier of fortune in the papal guard and under Italian princes before becoming a Genoese admiral in 1512. He beat Barbarossa off Civitavecchia in 1526 and commanded for France until June 1528, when he went over to Charles V, who made him his admiral; he then drove the French from Genoa and was hailed as its "Father of the Fatherland". He took Coron in 1532, shared command in the capture of Tunis in 1535, commanded the Holy League fleet at Preveza in 1538, was caught by the storm that wrecked the Algiers expedition of 1541, and took Mahdia in 1550, aged 84. He died in Genoa in 1560, aged 93.

## Command style

A careful admiral who would not risk fleets he partly owned himself; Venetian writers called him indecisive and averse to risk. At Preveza he refused to force the narrow mouth of the Gulf of Arta, tried to draw Barbarossa out to sea with a move south and with feints, and withdrew rather than fight under the Ottoman shore. Venetian and Papal sources accused him of sparing Barbarossa to weaken Venice; Spanish sources blamed the Venetians and the Pope's men for not following his orders.
