# Alexander the Great

## Summary

King of Macedon from 336 BCE and conqueror of the Achaemenid Empire. At [[gaugamela]] he led the right wing and the decisive cavalry thrust against a Persian army several times the size of his own.

## Career

He defeated [[darius-iii]] at Issus (333 BCE) and again at Gaugamela (331), then took Babylon and the other Persian capitals; Darius was murdered by [[bessus]] in 330, and Alexander later captured and executed Bessus. He met war elephants again in north-west India about five years after Gaugamela (Warfare History Network).

## Command style

Led the right with the [[companion-cavalry]], combining the [[macedonian-phalanx]] as an anvil with a cavalry hammer. At Gaugamela he screened his flanks with an angled double front, drifted right to open a gap, and recognised the moment to strike. He also turned back from the pursuit to rescue [[parmenion]] when the left was in danger, trading the chance to catch Darius for the army's safety.

## Battles

- [[gaugamela]] — commanded the right and the wedge against Darius.
