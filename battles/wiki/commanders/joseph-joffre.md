# Joseph Joffre

## Summary

Commander-in-chief of the French army from 1911 to December 1916, who lost the Battle of the Frontiers in August 1914 and then won the First Battle of the Marne.

## Career

An engineer officer who served in the siege of Paris in 1870–71 and in the colonies (Formosa, Indochina, Timbuktu, Madagascar), he became chief of the General Staff in 1911 and adopted Plan XVII, an immediate offensive into Lorraine and the Ardennes. Its failure in August 1914 cost the French army some 260,000 casualties in a month. Joffre kept his nerve during the retreat, sacked dozens of generals, moved troops by rail to form the 6th Army at Paris and launched the counter-offensive on the Marne on 5 September. He ran the costly offensives of 1915 in Artois and Champagne and planned the Somme; after the German surprise at Verdun he was eased out in December 1916, made Marshal of France, and later headed the French mission to the United States.

## Command style

Imperturbable and methodical, famous for his regular meals and sleep during the crisis of 1914. He believed in the offensive and in attrition, and was slow to see the strength of the defence in trench warfare.

## Battles

[[world-war-i]]: the Frontiers and the Marne (1914), the 1915 offensives, Verdun and the Somme (1916).
