# Douglas Haig

## Summary

Commander of the British Expeditionary Force on the Western Front from December 1915 to the end of the war, through the Somme, Passchendaele and the victories of 1918.

## Career

A cavalry officer who served in the Sudan and the Boer War, he commanded I Corps of the BEF in 1914 (Mons, the Aisne, First Ypres) and the First Army in 1915 (Neuve Chapelle, Loos). He replaced Sir John French as commander-in-chief in December 1915. He commanded the British at the Somme (1 July – 18 November 1916, where tanks were first used), Arras (1917), Third Ypres/Passchendaele (July–November 1917) and Cambrai (November 1917). After the German Spring Offensive broke his Fifth Army in March 1918 he issued his "backs to the wall" order in April and accepted Foch as supreme commander. From the Battle of Amiens on 8 August his armies won a series of victories, breaking the Hindenburg Line in September.

## Command style

Firm and optimistic, committed to wearing the German army down on the Western Front; his long offensives of 1916–17 cost enormous casualties and remain the most argued-over British command record of the war. His armies of 1918 combined infantry, artillery, tanks and aircraft with great skill.

## Battles

[[world-war-i]]: Ypres (1914), the Somme (1916), Passchendaele (1917), the Spring Offensive and the Hundred Days (1918).
