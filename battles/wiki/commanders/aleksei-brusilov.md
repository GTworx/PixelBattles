# Aleksei Brusilov

## Summary

Russian cavalry general whose offensive of June 1916 was the greatest Entente victory of the war until 1918 and nearly broke Austria-Hungary.

## Career

A veteran of the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78, he commanded the 8th Army in the Galician battles of 1914, taking Lemberg, and through the Carpathian fighting and the retreat of 1915. Given the Southwestern Front in March 1916, he attacked on 4 June along some 400 km at once, after short bombardments and close preparation of assault trenches, instead of massing on a narrow sector; the Austro-Hungarian 4th and 7th Armies collapsed and his front took about 400,000 prisoners. The other Russian fronts failed to support him, German reserves arrived, and by September the offensive had bogged down with very heavy Russian losses. He was briefly commander-in-chief under the Provisional Government in 1917, during the failed Kerensky offensive, and later served the Red Army.

## Command style

Innovative and practical: he hid his preparations, attacked everywhere to keep the enemy from shifting reserves, and trained his assault troops on full-size trench mock-ups.
