# Paul von Hindenburg

## Summary

Retired Prussian general recalled in August 1914 to command the 8th Army, who won Tannenberg with Ludendorff and from 1916 headed the German Supreme Army Command.

## Career

A veteran of Königgrätz (1866) and the Franco-Prussian War, he had retired in 1911. Recalled on 22 August 1914 with Erich Ludendorff as his chief of staff, he destroyed the Russian 2nd Army at Tannenberg and drove the 1st Army out of East Prussia at the Masurian Lakes, becoming a national hero. As commander in the east (Ober Ost) he directed the 1915 offensives that took Russian Poland. In August 1916 he replaced Falkenhayn as Chief of the General Staff, with Ludendorff as First Quartermaster-General; together they ran Germany's war effort and much of its government, backed unrestricted submarine warfare and the 1918 Spring Offensive. He stayed at the head of the army through the armistice and later served as President of Germany (1925–1934), appointing Hitler chancellor in 1933.

## Command style

A calm, imposing figurehead who left operational planning largely to Ludendorff and took responsibility for the decisions.

## Battles

[[world-war-i]]: Tannenberg and the Masurian Lakes (1914), the Eastern Front (1915), the Supreme Army Command (1916–1918).
