# Maharbal

## Summary

Carthaginian cavalry officer, associated with the Numidian horse at [[cannae]], best known for urging [[hannibal-barca]] to march on Rome after the victory.

## Career

His role at Cannae is uncertain: Livy places him in command of the Carthaginian right wing, Polybius never mentions him, and Appian gives the right wing to Hanno son of Bomilcar and Maharbal a cavalry reserve.

## Command style

An aggressive cavalryman. In Livy 22.51, Maharbal urges an immediate march on Rome (promising Hannibal would dine on the Capitol within five days), Hannibal asks for time to consider, and Maharbal answers 'Vincere scis, Hannibal; victoria uti nescis' (you know how to win a victory, not how to use one). Scholars regard the anecdote as doubtful: it survives in a corrupt manuscript tradition, Polybius omits it, Plutarch attributes a similar remark to a Carthaginian named Barca, and Hannibal lacked siege equipment and his army needed rest.
