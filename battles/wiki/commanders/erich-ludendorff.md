# Erich Ludendorff

## Summary

German staff officer, Hindenburg's partner from Tannenberg onwards and the effective director of Germany's war from 1916 to October 1918, who planned the 1918 Spring Offensive.

## Career

He led the coup de main that took the Liège citadel in August 1914, then became chief of staff to Hindenburg in East Prussia and planned Tannenberg with Max Hoffmann. As First Quartermaster-General from August 1916 he organised the Hindenburg Programme, the defensive withdrawal to the Hindenburg Line, the doctrine of elastic defence in depth and the treaty of Brest-Litovsk. With Russia out he launched the Spring Offensive of 21 March 1918 (Operation Michael and its successors) using storm troops and short bombardments; it gained ground but no decision. After Amiens he called 8 August "the black day of the German army"; at the end of September he demanded an armistice, and he was dismissed on 26 October 1918. After the war he joined Hitler's Beer Hall Putsch of 1923.

## Command style

A brilliant, tireless organiser and tactician whose operational goals in 1918 were vague; he told his staff to "punch a hole, and let the rest follow".

## Battles

[[world-war-i]]: Liège and Tannenberg (1914), the Eastern Front (1915), the Supreme Army Command (1916–1918), the Spring Offensive (1918).
