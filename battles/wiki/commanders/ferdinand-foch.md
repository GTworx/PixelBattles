# Ferdinand Foch

## Summary

French general and teacher of the offensive, who became Supreme Commander of the Allied armies in 1918 and directed the Hundred Days Offensive that ended the war.

## Career

Professor and then commandant of the École Supérieure de Guerre, he commanded the 9th Army at the Marne in 1914 (holding the St Gond marshes), coordinated the northern armies in the race to the sea and at First Ypres, and commanded the Northern Army Group on the Somme in 1916. Sidelined with Joffre in December 1916, he returned as chief of the General Staff in 1917. After the German breakthrough of 21 March 1918 he was given coordination of the Allied armies at Doullens (26 March) and made Supreme Allied Commander in April. He held the German offensives with a reserve, counter-attacked on the Marne on 18 July, and from August ran converging Allied offensives on the whole front. He was made Marshal of France in August 1918 and dictated the armistice terms at Compiègne on 11 November.

## Command style

Energetic and aggressive, preaching that a battle is lost only when one believes it lost; by 1918 his offensives were a series of short, limited blows on different sectors that kept the German reserves off balance.

## Battles

[[world-war-i]]: the Marne and Ypres (1914), the Somme (1916), the Second Marne and the Hundred Days (1918).
